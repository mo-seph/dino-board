Mosasauraboard

A circuit board in the shape of a mosasaurus, with lights along its back, touch pads, and a handful of sensors. It runs small programs called behaviours: one for each of its 18 lights, and any of them can be swapped for a new one.

Try it in the simulatorHave an AI write a behaviour

The boards are being made. Until they arrive, everything here runs on a pretend one in your browser, using the same program the real board will run.

What it can notice

  • Touch, in five places: snout, tail, front flipper, rear flipper, and a strip along its back that knows where your finger is.
  • Movement: which way up it is, tilting, shaking, tapping, spinning, and being in free fall.
  • Compass direction, and magnets nearby.
  • Temperature and humidity, at the tip of its tail.
  • Air pressure, finely enough to tell when it has been lifted from the floor to a table.
  • Light, at its nose.

What it can do

  • 18 lights along its back, any colour each, sitting above the touch strip so a light can follow your finger.
  • An eye, any colour.
  • Beep.
  • Remember a few numbers when switched off: a best score, a setting.

It has no screen, microphone, camera or proper speaker, and it doesn't know the time of day.

On the board, you choose a behaviour by holding the snout and sliding a finger along its back.

Think one up

Three sentences are enough: what you do to it (touch, tilt, shake, carry it somewhere, shine a torch at it), what it does back (which lights, what colours, any beeps), and what it's for (a game, an experiment, something useful).

"When I shake it, the lights fill up from the head like a battery charging, and slowly drain when I stop. If I fill it right up, the eye flashes and it beeps. It's a game: how fast can you fill it?"

Then give the idea to an AI model to turn into code, and try the result in the simulator.
